## About

I work with computational drug discovery and structural bioinformatics. My PhD in Biophysics at UFRJ (2025) was about developing a consensus scoring system for molecular docking based on artificial intelligence, which became OCDocker. During the sandwich period at the University of Cambridge, I started developing an automated system to analyse the impact that SNPs have on proteins, with a focus on the druggability of the target.

Before that, I did my MSc in Computational Modelling at the Federal University of Juiz de Fora, working on MHOLline 2.0, a web server for comparative modelling and analysis of proteins, which I kept maintaining at LNCC until 2022. My undergraduate research was on protein modelling by homology.

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at UFRJ and a remote collaborator at Cambridge. I also coordinate a pilot project on machine learning applied to electron microscopy images.

## Research interests

My work sits between computation and biology, and its general goal is to make the search for therapeutic compounds faster and cheaper. On the drug design side, I work with structure-based virtual screening, molecular docking and machine learning models that learn from docking scores and molecular descriptors of ligands and receptors. Earlier in my career I also worked on cheminformatics, including in silico ADME-Tox prediction for a national compound library.

On the biology side, I work with protein structure modelling and with the effect of genetic variants on protein stability and druggability, including scalable workflows for variant effect prediction. More recently I have been applying machine learning to electron microscopy images, in a pilot project that aims to widen the access of pathologists to this technique and to support image-based diagnosis.

## OCDocker

OCDocker (Open Consensus Docker) is the software I developed during my PhD, together with my supervisor Pedro H. M. Torres. It runs molecular docking with different programs and uses a predictive model, trained on the docking scores plus molecular descriptors of ligands and receptors, to produce a consensus score for virtual screening. In the benchmarks based on DUDE-Z and PDBbind, the model had a predictive power competitive with, and sometimes better than, the methods traditionally applied, and it was more effective at separating active from inactive compounds.

It is registered at INPI (BR512025007028-4).

Repository: [github.com/Arturossi/OCDocker](https://github.com/Arturossi/OCDocker)
